# Questions for the Owner

**Print this one.** It asks the same things as
[domain-interview.md](domain-interview.md), in the words you would actually say
out loud. That page is the engineering record — why each question matters and
what each answer costs us. This page is the one that goes in the folder.

Write the answers straight onto the paper. They get typed up afterwards.

---

## How to ask

- **Read each question as written.** Don't explain it first — an explanation
  tells them what you expect to hear, and they will agree with you.
- **Where there is an example with pesos in it, ask the example, not the rule.**
  "A farmer borrows ₱10,000 — how much does he pay back?" gets you a true answer
  in five seconds. "How is interest calculated?" gets you a shrug.
- **If the answer is "it depends", write down what it depends on.** That is
  usually the real answer, and it is the part that costs us money to find out
  late.
- **Ask the encoder separately from the owner**, and not in front of them. The
  person doing the daily encoding knows the exceptions and the workarounds; the
  owner knows the intent. They are often different.
- **Let silences run.** Don't fill them with a suggested answer.

## 1. The extra charge on a loan

*The old system calls this button "Compute Percentage" — they may call it the
porsyento, the tubo, or the patong. This is the most important section on the
sheet. Take your time, and ask every part.*

**A.** A farmer borrows ₱10,000 today and pays everything back three months
later. How much does he hand over?

**Answer:**

**B.** Same farmer, same ₱10,000 — but he pays back after one month instead.
How much now?

**Answer:**

**C.** Is the extra the same for every farmer, or do some of them get a lower
charge than others?

**Answer:**

**D.** A farmer owes ₱10,000 that he borrowed, plus ₱300 of charges from last
month that he has not paid. When you work out this month's charge, is it worked
out on the ₱10,000, or on the ₱10,300?

**Answer:**

**E.** When does the charge get added — on a fixed day every month, at the end
of a season, or whenever somebody decides to do it?

**Answer:**

**F.** A farmer borrowed for three months but settles after one. Do you charge
him for the three months, or only the one?

**Answer:**

**G.** Has there ever been a farmer you let off the charge completely? What was
the situation?

## 10. Things to take away with you

- [ ] **A copy of the farmer list out of the old system.** Ask for it on the
      day — we need to try it well before cutover, not on the morning itself.
- [ ] Printouts of: Current Advances · Return of Investment Status ·
      Recall Liquidated
- [ ] Photos of two screens we have never seen: "Payments From Customer" and
      "Compute Percentage"
- [ ] One blank carbonised advance slip — so the printing lines up
- [ ] The make and model of the printer
- [ ] Which computer is always switched on, and whether anyone turns it off at
      closing time
- [ ] How many people are encoding at the same time, and at what times of day
- [ ] What they do now for backups — and when a backup was last actually
      restored, not just taken
- [ ] **Would they buy one small dedicated computer** — about the size of a
      book — to run this on, so it is not sharing a machine somebody switches
      off? Worth asking even if the answer is no today.

## Afterwards

Write the answers into `docs/domain-rules.md` and commit it. Copy each one back
into the matching **Answer:** line in
[domain-interview.md](domain-interview.md) as well, so the reasoning and the
answer sit together — including the answers that changed nothing. "We asked, and
it stays as it is" is worth recording, because it stops the question being
reopened from scratch in six months.
